# Spoken Danish selection

90 original bilingual teaching entries across nine daily conversation situations; 18 extra coarse/informal comprehension entries in a separate manual reference. These are editorial examples, not corpus frequency ranks or a complete slang inventory. Original examples and Russian translations are composed for this application; dictionary examples were not bulk copied.

Primary language checks:

- Dansk Sprognævn, Retskrivningsordbogen §6.6: https://ro.dsn.dk/?paragraf=6&side=6&type=rulesearch . An apostrophe can represent omitted letters when reproducing pronunciation, including hva', ik'/ikk', ku'. This does not prescribe that every Danish speaker uses the same reduction.
- Det Danske Sprog- og Litteraturselskab, Den Danske Ordbog: https://ordnet.dk/ddo/ordbog?query=s%C3%A5dan . The entry identifies så'n as a speech-imitating written form; it is displayed separately from standard sådan.
- https://ordnet.dk/ddo/ordbog/have_2 , https://ordnet.dk/ddo/ordbog/give , https://ordnet.dk/ddo/ordbog/skulle : standard constructions and speech variants.
- https://ordnet.dk/ddo/ordbog/eddermame : a reinforcing swear word, especially spoken and associated with eastern Denmark. The application retains that regional qualification.
- Register/sense checks use corresponding DDO headwords including fandeme, kraftedeme, sateme, skide, skride, lort, forbandet, pokker and sgu. Strength labels are teaching approximations: tone, target, relationship and setting affect force.

`da` retains standard spelling for answers and existing system TTS. `heard` is display-only, not IPA, a complete pronunciation rule or an instruction to the voice engine. System TTS does not reproduce all native connected speech. Eighteen short conversation lessons reuse these same entry identities; their progress survives reopening. Coarse expressions are excluded from the ordinary training catalogue and daily scheduler.
