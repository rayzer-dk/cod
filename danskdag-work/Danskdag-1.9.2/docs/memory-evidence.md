# Evidence behind memory practice

Reviewed 2026-10-23. These sources support the direction of the practice design. They do not validate Danskdag itself, Russian–Danish learning outcomes, its exact review intervals, or its new-item limit. None establishes one universally best language-learning method.

## Broader language-learning framework

Nation (2007), *The Four Strands*, is a course-design framework, not an experiment proving an optimal app feature mix. It calls for meaning-focused input, meaning-focused output, deliberate language study, and fluency development. [Publisher reference and abstract](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/abs/10.2167/illt039.0); [university repository](https://openaccess.wgtn.ac.nz/articles/journal_contribution/The_four_strands/12552167); DOI: [10.2167/illt039.0](https://doi.org/10.2167/illt039.0).

Implication: word reviews should complement reading, listening, meaningful speaking/writing, and fluent reuse. Existing examples and controlled gap fills can connect form with use, but a gap fill is not by itself meaning-focused conversation or proof of transfer. The framework's suggested course balance is not a required exact percentage for this app.

## Product decisions and honest measurement

- Due reviews before new material, at most two new items per batch, the 10-minute retry, and the 1/3/7/14/30-day ladder are explicit product heuristics. The studies above do not validate those numbers.
- An answer shown before a correct response is assisted practice. It should not advance a claim of independent retention; schedule another opportunity to retrieve it.
- Multiple choice and word-bank building can help practice. They should not be reported as unaided Danish production. Typed recall can assess production of the accepted form, but does not measure spontaneous speech, broad comprehension, or all valid paraphrases.
- Measure delayed success only when time has actually elapsed. In-session retries, total tasks, XP, and streaks measure activity or performance in that setting.
- Use existing, reviewed examples for contextual practice. Do not imply a single sentence necessarily produces more vocabulary learning than a word pair; that outcome depends on task and learner conditions.
- No measured improvement in Danish retention is claimed until learning outcomes are evaluated. This is a research-informed implementation whose learning outcomes have not yet been experimentally evaluated.


## Additional check on 2026-10-24

Kim & Webb (2022), The Effects of Spaced Practice on Second Language Learning: A Meta-Analysis, Language Learning 72,269–319, https://doi.org/10.1111/lang.12479, synthesizes 48 experiments with 3,411 participants. It supports distributed practice for second-language learning; equal and expanding schedules were statistically equivalent overall. This supports retaining spaced retrieval rather than labelling this app's increasing intervals uniquely optimal.

Karatas et al., Improving second language vocabulary learning and retention by leveraging memory enhancement techniques: A multidomain pedagogical approach, Language Teaching Research 29(1),112–149 (2025 issue; first online 2021), https://doi.org/10.1177/13621688211053525, compared a combined retrieval/feedback/spacing regimen with an initial-study-and-cramming regimen. Benefits of the combined regimen do not isolate every component or validate Russian-Danish app outcomes.

These checks do not establish that a particular new scheduling algorithm, images or a fixed quantity of cards guarantees conversational fluency. The app applies retrieval, spacing, contextual examples, finite comparison exercises and assisted-answer accounting; it has not been evaluated as a complete language course.
