## Linguistic decisions

- **fyr:** en fyr covers both an informal word for a man and a pine. Their definite forms differ: fyren versus fyrren. Et fyr covers both a lighthouse and a heating installation. Fyr as wood is normally a material name without an article.
- **leder / lider:** these are distinct words and constructions, rather than several meanings of one verb. The family compares en leder, lede, lede efter, lide af and kunne lide.
- **synes / tror:** personal evaluation versus an assumption about a verifiable fact is a useful learning cue, not an absolute boundary between the verbs.
- **kommer til at:** accidental action and future consequence share the same surface construction; examples make the intended reading explicit.
- **for / får:** spelling distinguishes a preposition, adverb or conjunction from the present tense of få. Et får is also a noun. For as a conjunction takes main-clause word order.
- **skål:** the vessel has plural skåle; a toast has plural skåler. Skål! is an interjection.
- **bank:** the financial institution is common gender, en bank. A knock is neuter, et bank. Få bank uses a plural noun and can refer to a sporting defeat or to physical blows; the example uses the sporting sense.
- **jo, vel, nok, lige, bare:** the Russian labels describe the function in the particular example. They should not be presented as universal one-word equivalents. Jo as a positive response to a negative question often has no simple Russian particle equivalent.
- **skal / vil:** both can concern the future. The selection distinguishes obligation, arrangement, desire and prediction, rather than mapping them to fixed Russian tense forms.
- **Word classes:** the property identifies the relevant word inside the form. Thus ret in have ret and bank in få bank are nouns; nok in skal nok and vel in lev vel remain adverbs.
- No Cyrillic phonetic spellings or invented pronunciations are included.

## Structural validation

The module imports successfully, all 141 family/sense IDs are unique, every family contains 2–6 senses, and all 113 Danish examples are unique after case/punctuation normalization. Russian labels and accepted alternatives do not collide within a family. The examples were also checked against content.mjs, curriculum.mjs and word-classes-data.mjs with zero collisions.
